## Climate and Environment

*This endeavor is unlike the other four: it is about fixing the past rather than improving the future — humanity messed up the Earth, and we now have to clean up our mess if we are to survive into a better future.*

*Narrowed scope: only the KPI and the milestone "The Bend" are assessed; "The Balance", "The Ceiling" and all Open Challenges are skipped. Recent NOAA values are preliminary.*

**KPI: Atmospheric CO₂ concentration (ppm) and 10-year trend (ppm/year)** — 🔴 Worsening. Concentration: **432.34 ppm** (Mauna Loa (MLO) monthly mean, May 2026; an all-time monthly record; +1.83 ppm on May 2025 (430.51)). 10-year trend, 2016–2025 (Jan→Dec): **2.56 ppm/yr** (MLO) and 2.53 ppm/yr (NOAA global mean).

Year-to-year growth in CO₂ concentration slowed: at MLO it was +2.23 ppm in 2025 (Jan→Dec), down from a record +3.33 ppm in 2024, and monthly values for Jan–May 2026 were +1.48 to +2.26 ppm above the same months of 2025. The Met Office forecasts a slower 2026 annual-mean rise (+2.37 ± 0.55 ppm, Scripps basis), which it attributes to La Niña-like conditions. Separately, on emissions inventories: growth of IEA energy CO₂ slowed to about +0.4% in 2025, "the slowest rate since 2021". On a different measure, GCB total CO₂ (fossil + land-use change) was slightly lower in 2025 (42.2 GtCO₂) than in 2024 (42.4 GtCO₂), which GCB attributes to lower land-use emissions as El Niño ended. Global fossil power generation fell 0.2% in 2025, the first time since 2020 that it did not rise. Renewables overtook coal in global electricity generation in 2025, with a 33.8% share against 33.0% for coal.

**However:** the MLO monthly mean CO₂ concentration is at a record, and there is no structural slowdown: the 10-year mean slipped from 2.64 to 2.56 ppm/yr for a mechanical reason (the El Niño year 2015 left the window), while the MLO half-decade mean rose from 2.51 ppm/yr (2016–20) to 2.61 ppm/yr (2021–25). IPCC C1 scenarios need a 2020s decadal average of 1.33–1.79 ppm/yr, and the Met Office says even the forecast 2026 rise is "too fast to track IPCC 1.5°C scenarios". On emissions, fossil CO₂ set a record of 38.1 GtCO₂ in 2025 (+1.0%, GCB), and IEA energy CO₂ and Carbon Monitor fossil+industry CO₂ also set 2025 records. China's CO₂ still rose 2% year on year in Q1 2026. Any 2026 dip looks shock-driven: after the Hormuz oil-supply shock, the IEA forecasts that oil demand will fall in 2026 but rebound above its 2025 level in 2027. GCB puts the remaining 1.5°C (50%) budget from the start of 2026 at 170 GtCO₂, about 4 years at 2025 emission levels.
