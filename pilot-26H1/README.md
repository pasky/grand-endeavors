## Climate and Environment

*Unlike the other four endeavors, this one is about fixing the past rather than improving the future: humanity messed up the Earth, and we now have to clean up our mess if we are to survive into a better future.*

*Narrowed scope: only the KPI and the milestone "The Bend" are assessed; "The Balance", "The Ceiling" and all Open Challenges are skipped. Recent NOAA values are preliminary.*

**KPI: Atmospheric CO₂ concentration (ppm) and 10-year trend (ppm/year)** — concentration **432.34 ppm** (Mauna Loa monthly mean, May 2026; an all-time monthly record); 10-year trend, 2016–2025 (Jan→Dec): **2.56 ppm/yr** (MLO) and **2.53 ppm/yr** (NOAA global mean). Assessment: 🔴 Worsening.

Year-to-year growth in CO₂ concentration slowed: MLO rose +2.23 ppm in 2025 (Jan→Dec), down from +3.33 ppm in 2024, and January–June 2026 monthly values were +1.48 to +2.26 ppm above the same months of 2025. The Met Office forecasts a 2026 rise slowed by La Niña-like conditions. Separately, on emissions inventories, growth is slowing towards a plateau: IEA energy CO₂ grew about +0.4% in 2025, "the slowest rate since 2021"; on GCB's measure, total CO₂ (fossil + land-use change) grew 0.3%/yr over 2015–2024, down from 1.9%/yr over 2005–2014. Fossil power generation fell 0.2% in 2025, the first time since 2020 that it did not rise, as clean power (+887 TWh) grew by more than demand (+849 TWh). On IEA energy CO₂, China fell 0.5% and India 0.1% (the IEA says India's fall was "largely due to cyclical factors resulting from the strong monsoon"). Renewables overtook coal in global electricity in 2025, with a 33.8% share against 33.0% for coal.

**However:** CO₂ concentration is at a record — the May 2026 MLO monthly mean is the highest on record, the record MLO daily mean was 433.95 ppm (1 May 2026), and the 2025 annual means (427.35 ppm MLO / 425.62 ppm global) were both records. The section calls the concentration-growth slowdown cyclical, not a change in the trend: MLO half-decade means rose from 2.51 ppm/yr (2016–20) to 2.61 ppm/yr (2021–25), while IPCC C1 (1.5°C) scenarios need a 2020s decadal average of 1.33–1.79 ppm/yr, and the Met Office says the 2026 rise will still be "too fast to track IPCC 1.5°C scenarios". On emissions, fossil CO₂ set a record of 38.1 GtCO₂ in 2025 (+1.0%, GCB), and IEA energy CO₂ and Carbon Monitor fossil+industry CO₂ also set records. China's CO₂ rose 2% year on year in Q1 2026. Any 2026 dip would be driven by the Hormuz oil shock, and the IEA expects oil demand to rebound in 2027. GCB puts the remaining 1.5°C (50%) budget from the start of 2026 at 170 GtCO₂, about 4 years at 2025 emission levels.
