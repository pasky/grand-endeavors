# Five Grand Endeavors — 26H1 Report

> *Science has a communication problem. It reports isolated discoveries, not progress towards the big outcomes. This newsletter updates humanity on the progress of science towards such outcomes — just as a company would report its progress to its board.*

This edition covers the reporting period 26H1 (Jan–Jun 2026, covering developments published up to 2026-07-14). It covers one endeavor: **Climate and Environment**. Within that section, the KPI and the milestone "The Bend" were assessed this period, while "The Balance" and "The Ceiling" carry forward their 2026-01-03 assessments (not re-assessed this period) and the Open Challenges were not assessed. ## Climate and Environment

*This endeavor is unlike the other four: it is about fixing the past rather than improving the future. Many historic civilizations fell to climate change, and we now have to clean up our mess if we are to survive into a better future.*

**KPI: Atmospheric CO₂ concentration (ppm) and 10-year trend (ppm/year)** — **431.43 ppm** (MLO monthly mean, June 2026; preliminary; +1.82 ppm on June 2025); 10-year trend **2.56 ppm/yr** (MLO, 2016–2025; same observation as the previous reading, no new data) — 🔴 Worsening (previously 🟡)

Global fossil power generation fell in 2025 by 0.2% (-38 TWh), the first time since 2020 that it did not rise; low-carbon generation (+887 TWh) outpaced demand growth (+849 TWh). Renewables overtook coal in global electricity generation in 2025: 33.8% (10,730 TWh) versus 33.0% (10,476 TWh). Emissions growth is slowing: IEA energy-related CO₂ rose by around 0.4% in 2025, "the slowest rate since 2021", and China's 2025 CO₂ was roughly flat (estimates of -0.3% from Carbon Brief/CREA, -0.5% from IEA and +0.4% from GCB). Separately, year-on-year growth in CO₂ concentration slowed: Mauna Loa (MLO) monthly gains for Jan–Jun 2026 were +1.48 to +2.26 ppm, below the May-over-May gains of 2023–2025 (+3.03, +2.90, +3.61 ppm) — only the growth rate slowed, and concentrations still set records.

**However:** the MLO monthly mean CO₂ concentration hit a record **432.34 ppm in May 2026** (+1.83 ppm on May 2025), and the MLO daily mean reached 433.95 ppm on 1 May 2026, the highest in NOAA's MLO daily record. The 10-year MLO concentration trend of 2.56 ppm/yr is far above the 1.33-1.79 ppm/yr 2020s average that IPCC 1.5°C pathways require; its dip from 2.64 ppm/yr is mechanical, and MLO half-decade means still rose (2.51 → 2.61 ppm/yr). On emissions, 2025 set records on every fossil/energy CO₂ basis: GCB fossil CO₂ 38.1 GtCO₂ (+1.0%), IEA energy-related CO₂ 38,082 MtCO₂ and Carbon Monitor fossil + industry CO₂ 37.2 GtCO₂ (only GCB total CO₂, which includes land-use change, dipped slightly). China's CO₂ rose 2% year on year in Q1 2026. The GCB puts the remaining budget for a 50% chance of limiting warming to 1.5°C at 170 GtCO₂ from the start of 2026, about 4 years at 2025 emission levels. The IEA *projects* global oil demand to fall by 1.1 mb/d in 2026 after the Hormuz shock, then rise to 105.3 mb/d in 2027.
