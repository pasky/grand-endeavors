# Five Grand Endeavors — 26H1 Report

> *Science has a communication problem. It reports isolated discoveries, not progress towards the big outcomes. This newsletter updates humanity on the progress of science towards such outcomes — just as a company would report its progress to its board.*

This edition covers the reporting period 26H1 (2026-01-01 to 2026-06-30, covering developments published up to 2026-07-14). It covers one endeavor: **Climate and Environment**. Within that section, the KPI and the milestone "The Bend" were assessed this period; "The Balance" and "The Ceiling" carry forward their 2026-01-03 assessments (not re-assessed this period), and the Open Challenges were not assessed. This edition does **not** cover Robots and Automation (Software and Hardware), Rockets and Space, Fusion and Energy, Health and Lifespan, or the Supplemental sections (The Knowledge Beyond; Society and Cohesion): no section reports were produced for them this period.

## Climate and Environment

*This endeavor is unlike the other four: it is about fixing the past rather than improving the future. Many historic civilizations fell to climate change, and we now have to clean up our mess if we are to survive into a better future.*

**KPI: Atmospheric CO₂ concentration (ppm) and 10-year trend (ppm/year)** — **431.43 ppm** (MLO monthly mean, June 2026; published 2026-07-07, preliminary; year ago 429.61 ppm in June 2025, **+1.82 ppm**); 10-year trend **2.56 ppm/yr** (MLO, mean of NOAA Jan 1 → Dec 31 growth rates 2016–2025; obs 2025, published 2026-01-10; unchanged, no new data) — **🔴 Worsening** (previously 🟡)

Global fossil power generation fell by 0.2% (-38 TWh) in 2025, measured rather than forecast, as low-carbon generation (+887 TWh) outpaced electricity demand growth (+849 TWh). Renewables overtook coal as a source of electricity: 33.8% (10,730 TWh) versus 33.0% (10,476 TWh) of global generation. Emissions growth has slowed to a plateau: the IEA puts 2025 energy-related CO₂ growth at around 0.4%, "the slowest rate since 2021", and China and India were roughly flat in 2025. Separately, the year-on-year rise in CO₂ concentration eased: each month of January–June 2026 at Mauna Loa (MLO) was +1.48 to +2.26 ppm above the same month a year earlier, versus May year-on-year gains of +3.03, +2.90 and +3.61 ppm in 2023–2025.

**However:** CO₂ concentration hit new records: an MLO monthly mean of **432.34 ppm in May 2026** and an MLO daily mean of 433.95 ppm on 1 May 2026. The 10-year MLO trend of 2.56 ppm/yr shows no structural slowdown; its dip from 2.64 ppm/yr (2015–2024) is mechanical, and MLO half-decade means still rose, from 2.51 ppm/yr (2016–2020) to 2.61 ppm/yr (2021–2025). 2025 emissions were a record on every fossil/energy CO₂ estimate: GCB fossil CO₂ 38.1 GtCO₂ (+1.0%), IEA energy-related CO₂ 38,082 MtCO₂, Carbon Monitor fossil + industry CO₂ 37.2 GtCO₂ (only GCB total CO₂, which includes land-use change, dipped: 42.2 GtCO₂ versus 42.4 GtCO₂ in 2024). China's CO₂ rose 2% year on year in Q1 2026. The Met Office says even its forecast 2026 rise in Mauna Loa CO₂ is "too fast to track IPCC 1.5°C scenarios", and the GCB puts the remaining carbon budget for a 50% chance of limiting warming to 1.5°C at 170 GtCO₂ from the start of 2026, about 4 years at 2025 emission levels.
