## Climate and Environment

*This endeavor is unlike the other four: it is about fixing the past rather than improving the future — we now have to clean up our mess if we are to survive into a better future.*

*Narrowed scope: this run covers only the KPI and the milestone "The Bend"; "The Balance", "The Ceiling" and all Open Challenges are skipped.*

**KPI: Atmospheric CO₂ concentration (ppm) and 10-year trend (ppm/year)** — concentration **432.34 ppm** (Mauna Loa monthly mean, May 2026; an all-time monthly record); 10-year trend, 2016–2025: **2.56 ppm/yr (MLO)** and **2.53 ppm/yr (NOAA global mean)**. KPI assessment: 🔴 Worsening.

Short-term growth in CO₂ concentration slowed: the 2025 rise was +2.23 ppm at Mauna Loa (Jan→Dec), down from +3.33 in 2024. The UK Met Office attributes the slowdown to La Niña-like conditions that temporarily strengthened natural carbon sinks. (Concentration growth is not an emissions measure; the section draws no emissions conclusion from it.) Separately, on emissions inventories, growth has slowed towards a plateau: GCB total CO₂ (fossil + land-use change) grew 0.3%/yr over 2015–2024, compared with 1.9%/yr over 2005–2014, and was slightly lower in 2025 (42.2 GtCO₂) than in 2024 (42.4). Fossil power generation fell 0.2% (−38 TWh) in 2025, the first time since 2020 that it did not rise; clean power grew by 887 TWh, more than the 849 TWh growth in demand. On IEA energy CO₂, China fell 0.5% and India 0.1% in 2025 (India's fall "largely due to cyclical factors resulting from the strong monsoon", per the IEA). Renewables overtook coal in global electricity in 2025: a 33.8% share, against 33.0% for coal.

**However:** CO₂ concentration keeps setting records: the Mauna Loa record daily value was 433.95 ppm (1 May 2026), and the 2025 annual means (427.35 ppm MLO / 425.62 ppm global) were both records. The 10-year concentration growth trend shows no structural slowdown: the MLO half-decade mean rose from 2.51 ppm/yr (2016–20) to 2.61 ppm/yr (2021–25), and IPCC C1 (1.5°C) scenarios need a 2020s decadal average of 1.33–1.79 ppm/yr; the Met Office says the rise is still "too fast to track IPCC 1.5°C scenarios". On emissions, every fossil or energy CO₂ estimate published in 26H1 puts 2025 at a record: fossil CO₂ 38.1 GtCO₂, +1.0% (GCB); energy CO₂ 38,082 Mt, about +0.4% (IEA); fossil + industry CO₂ 37.2 Gt, +0.7% (Carbon Monitor). China's CO₂ rose 2% year on year in Q1 2026. Any 2026 dip would be shock-driven: after the Strait of Hormuz supply disruption, the IEA forecasts 2026 oil demand to fall by 1.1 mb/d, then rebound by 2 mb/d, to 105.3 mb/d, in 2027. GCB puts the remaining 1.5°C (50%) carbon budget from the start of 2026 at 170 GtCO₂, about 4 years at 2025 emission levels.
