# KPI: Atmospheric CO2 concentration (ppm) + 10-year trend (ppm/yr)

Reporting period: 26H1 (Jan–Jun 2026), as of end of June 2026.
Primary source: NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory (Mauna Loa + global marine
surface network), Scripps/NOAA Keeling record. Measure = atmospheric CO2 dry-air
mole fraction (ppm) — NOT emissions. Keep distinct from "The Bend" emissions data.

## 26H1 annual peak

- The Mauna Loa seasonal CO2 maximum occurs each year in May. The **26H1 annual
  peak = May 2026 monthly mean of 432.34 ppm** (highest monthly value of the
  Jan–Jun window; subsequent months decline seasonally). 2026 monthly progression:
  Jan 428.62, Feb 429.35, Mar 430.15, Apr 431.12, May 432.34 ppm.
  https://gml.noaa.gov/webdata/ccgg/trends/co2/co2_mm_mlo.txt

- Global (marine-surface) monthly mean lags and is lower: **March 2026 = 428.60
  ppm** (latest available global monthly, updated Jun 05, 2026; global figures run
  ~2 months behind Mauna Loa).
  https://gml.noaa.gov/ccgg/trends/global.html

## 10-year trend (ppm/year average growth rate) — the README KPI component

- Mauna Loa annual mean growth rate, 10-yr average 2016–2025 = **2.57 ppm/yr**
  (concentration-difference method 2015→2025: 2.63 ppm/yr).
  Global annual mean growth rate, 10-yr average 2016–2025 = **2.54 ppm/yr**
  (concentration method 2.60 ppm/yr). So the decade trend ≈ **~2.6 ppm/yr**, well
  above the 1960s (~0.8) / 2000s (~2.0) decadal averages — the rise is accelerating.
  Mauna Loa growth-rate data file:
  https://gml.noaa.gov/webdata/ccgg/trends/co2/co2_gr_mlo.txt
  Growth-rate methodology page:
  https://gml.noaa.gov/ccgg/trends/gr.html

- 2024 set a RECORD annual jump (global +3.74 ppm/yr — largest in the record;
  Mauna Loa +3.33), driven by El Niño + wildfire emissions reducing land/ocean
  uptake. 2025 fell back to +2.23 (ML) / +2.13 (global) ppm/yr — i.e. CO2 still
  RISING, just not at the 2024 record pace.
  Global growth-rate data file:
  https://gml.noaa.gov/webdata/ccgg/trends/co2/co2_gr_gl.txt

## STATUS: WORSENING

Atmospheric CO2 continues to climb to new record highs every year — Mauna Loa
hit 432.34 ppm in May 2026 (the 26H1 peak), and the ~2.6 ppm/yr 10-year trend is
the steepest decade on record. Even though emissions growth is decelerating ("The
Bend"), concentrations keep rising because emissions remain net-positive; the KPI
will keep worsening until emissions fall to near net-zero.
