# Milestone: "The Bend": is the year of peak global GHG emissions definitively behind us? (26H1, Jan–Jun 2026)

Metric boundaries used below. Do not mix them:
- **Fossil CO2** (GCB) = fossil fuels + cement, net of cement carbonation.
- **Energy CO2** (IEA) = fuel combustion + industrial processes.
- **Fossil+industry CO2** (Carbon Monitor).
- **Total CO2** (GCB) = fossil + land-use change.
- **Total GHG, CO2e** (EDGAR, Climate TRACE).

All 2025 numbers are preliminary.

## 2025 global emissions: final/updated estimates published in 26H1

- **GCB 2025 (final paper, published in ESSD on 13 May 2026):** fossil CO2 was **38.1 GtCO2 in 2025, up 1.0% (range 0.2% to 1.7%) on 2024**. The paper calls this "an historical record high". Coal, oil and gas emissions each rose (+1.0%, +1.1% and +1.3%).
  https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/18/3211/2026/
  - Spot-check, 28 Sep 2026 (ESSD page): "grew by 1.0 % in 2025 (0.2 % to 1.7 %) to 10.4 GtC (38.1 GtCO2), an historical record high"; "Emissions from coal, oil and gas in 2025 are all expected to be above their 2024 levels (by 1.0 %, 1.1 % and 1.3 % respectively)"; total CO2 "42.4 GtCO2 yr−1 in 2024, with a slightly lower preliminary estimate of ... 42.2 GtCO2 yr−1 for 2025"; remaining 1.5 °C budget "50 GtC, 170 GtCO2 left, equivalent to around 4 years at the 2025 emissions levels".
- **Change vs pilot-2025:** pilot-2025/climate.md reported the Nov 2025 GCB projection of 38.1 GtCO2 (+1.1%) for 2025. The final ESSD paper keeps 38.1 GtCO2 and gives +1.0%.
- **GCB total CO2 (fossil + land use), same paper:** 42.4 GtCO2 in 2024. The preliminary 2025 figure is "slightly lower" at **42.2 GtCO2**, "due to lower net land-use emissions"; the land-use decrease (to ~4.1 GtCO2) is "mainly attributable to the end of the El Niño conditions" (verified on the ESSD page, Sep 2026). This is a land-use fluctuation, not a structural fossil decline. Total emissions grew **0.3%/yr over 2015–2024**, compared with 1.9%/yr over 2005–2014. The remaining 1.5°C (50%) budget from the start of 2026 is **170 GtCO2, about 4 years** at 2025 emission levels.
  https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/18/3211/2026/
- **IEA Global Energy Review 2026 (April 2026):** energy CO2 rose **about 0.4% in 2025 to 38,082 Mt**, a new record. This is the slowest growth since 2021. Including flaring, the total is "nearly 38.4 Gt". By region (Mt, change vs 2024):
  - China 12,718 (**−0.5%**)
  - India 3,114 (−0.1%, the first fall on record under normal economic conditions; the IEA says this was "largely due to cyclical factors resulting from the strong monsoon", p. 13)
  - USA 4,606 (+2.2%)
  - EU 2,372 (−0.8%)
  - Advanced economies rose 0.5%, their first increase since 2018 excluding the post-Covid rebound.

  https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/df903e1c-49c6-4757-8cbf-6fbcfe7611a0/GlobalEnergyReview2026.pdf (PDF pp. 13–14, 36, 45)

  - p. 13: "In advanced economies, emissions rose by 0.5%, the first annual increase since 2018 (excluding the post-Covid rebound)."

- **Carbon Monitor (Nature Reviews Earth & Environment, 14 Apr 2026):** fossil+industry CO2 reached **a record 37.2 Gt in 2025, up 0.7%**. Global **power-sector emissions fell 0.9%**. The authors say China and India "entered an emission plateau", while the US and EU rebounded. The Carbon Monitor news page gives 37.3 Gt.
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s43017-026-00780-4

- **Ember Global Electricity Review 2026 (21 Apr 2026):** global **fossil power generation fell 0.2% (−38 TWh) in 2025**: "For the first time since the Covid-19 pandemic in 2020, and only the fifth time this century, fossil generation did not rise". ## Major emitters: China

- **China, full-year 2025 (Carbon Brief/CREA, 12 Feb 2026):** CO2 fell **0.3%** and has been "flat or falling" for 21 months since March 2024. Q4 2025 was down 1%. Carbon Brief says whether China has passed its peak remains an open question.
  https://www.carbonbrief.org/analysis-chinas-co2-emissions-have-now-been-flat-or-falling-for-21-months
- **Sources disagree on China's 2025 direction.** GCB projects **+0.4% (range −0.1% to +0.9%)** for China's fossil CO2, while the IEA estimates −0.5% and CREA −0.3%. In other words, China is on a plateau, not in a confirmed decline.
  https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/18/3211/2026/
- **China, Q1 2026 (Carbon Brief/CREA, 4 Jun 2026): a setback.** CO2 rose **2% year on year**. Power-sector emissions rose 4% because of "wasted" (curtailed) wind and solar. Emissions nevertheless "remain below the peak in March 2024".
  https://www.carbonbrief.org/analysis-chinas-co2-climbs-2-in-early-2026-due-to-wasted-wind-and-solar
- **China's official statistics are uncertain (Carbon Brief, 26 May 2026).** China appears to have changed the scope of its carbon-intensity metric retroactively. As a result, official figures now imply CO2 rose only 7% over 2020–2025, compared with 14% under the previous statistics. That leaves a gap of about 700–730 MtCO2 per year.
  https://www.carbonbrief.org/analysis-chinas-new-carbon-metric-leaves-germany-sized-gap-in-its-emissions
  - Spot-check, 28 Sep 2026: the article says China "has now made what appears to be a retrospective change" (China has not officially announced it). It gives the gap as "700m tonnes of CO2 (MtCO2) per year" in the summary and "730m tonnes" in the body (1,430 MtCO2 rise under the old statistics vs 690 MtCO2 implied by the new metric). The section keeps the "appears" hedge.

## 2026 outlook in 26H1: the Hormuz (Iran-war) energy shock

- **IEA Oil Market Report (17 Jun 2026):** 2026 global oil demand is forecast to **fall by 1.1 mb/d**, after Q2 deliveries dropped 5 mb/d year on year. However, demand is forecast to **rebound by 2 mb/d to 105.3 mb/d in 2027**. That would be above the implied 2025 level, so any 2026 dip is expected to be temporary rather than a structural peak.
  https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-june-2026
  - Spot-check, 28 Sep 2026: the page says demand is "forecast to decline by 1.1 mb/d y-o-y in 2026", that "2Q26 deliveries plunged by 5 mb/d y-o-y", and that demand is "projected to rise by a relatively modest 2 mb/d to 105.3 mb/d" in 2027.
  - The implied 2025 level is computed from those figures: 105.3 − 2 = 103.3 mb/d in 2026, and 103.3 + 1.1 = about 104.4 mb/d in 2025.
- **Ember via Carbon Brief (28 Apr 2026):** the "return to coal" is limited. In a worst case, global coal power rises no more than **1.8% in 2026**, and CREA data showed no return to coal as of March.
  https://www.carbonbrief.org/world-will-not-see-significant-return-to-coal-in-2026-despite-iran-crisis/
- **Expert view at the start of the period (Z. Hausfather, 5 Jan 2026):** "global emissions have yet to decline (even if they have plateaued)."
  https://www.theclimatebrink.com/p/keep-it-in-the-ground

## Status

**Approaching (not achieved).** Every 26H1 estimate puts **2025 at a new record high**:
- fossil CO2 +1.0% (GCB)
- energy CO2 +0.4% (IEA)
- fossil+industry CO2 +0.7% (Carbon Monitor)
- total GHG +0.7% (EDGAR, published after the period)

Growth has slowed to a plateau: fossil power generation fell in 2025, and China and India are flat. A 2026 dip now looks plausible, but it is driven by a shock (Hormuz), and the IEA forecasts an oil rebound in 2027. So the peak year is **not yet definitively behind us**.
